Depth and verbosity
Claude's profile is ~2.5x longer (438 lines vs. ~227) and treats each of the 11 capabilities individually with dedicated subsections throughout; Gemini's is more consolidated/narrative, closer to an executive-readable document. Neither is objectively "right" — it's a real tradeoff between traceability and readability.

Confidence tagging discipline
This is the most consequential difference. Claude tags almost every individual claim — a paragraph will have 3-4 separate **Confirmed**/**Inferred**/**Unknown** tags, each scoped to one specific assertion (e.g., "operation confirmed, but whether the doc is actually deleted is Unknown"). Gemini tags at the paragraph/bullet level — one **Confirmed** covering several compounded claims, some of which are more architecturally-inferred than directly evidenced. The contract asks for tagging at the claim level, and Claude followed that more faithfully.

Evidence References (Section 14) — Gemini's weakest section
Claude's Section 14 reconstructs real file:line citations per capability (pulled from what the capability outputs already cited) and explicitly flags which claims have no traceable citation. Gemini's Section 14 is essentially a disclaimer: "citations were not present... claims are traceable back to a specific capability output" — no actual file/line reconstruction attempted. For a knowledge-corpus deliverable meant to support engineering decisions, this is a meaningful gap in Gemini's output.
