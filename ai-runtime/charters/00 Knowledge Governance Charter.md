# Knowledge Governance Charter

**Version:** 0.1 (Working Draft)

**Status:** Living Document

---

# Purpose

The purpose of this charter is to establish the principles governing the creation, management, protection and use of knowledge throughout the OSkey Knowledge Pipeline.

The objective is not simply to protect information, but to enable trusted collaboration between humans and AI systems while preserving intellectual property, confidentiality, evidence and organisational trust.

This charter applies equally to human contributors, AI systems and future autonomous agents.

---

# Vision

Knowledge is a strategic business asset.

The Knowledge Pipeline exists to transform organisational understanding into reusable, trustworthy knowledge that supports product development, engineering, operations, governance and future AI orchestration.

The knowledge itself should become more valuable over time.

---

# Guiding Principles

## Knowledge before documents

The objective is not to produce documentation.

The objective is to create reliable organisational knowledge.

Documents are one representation of that knowledge.

---

## Evidence before confidence

Knowledge should be supported by evidence wherever practical.

Confidence should never replace evidence.

AI systems should distinguish between:

* Confirmed
* Inferred
* Unknown

---

## Humans govern

Humans remain accountable for significant architectural, security and business decisions.

AI assists reasoning.

AI does not become the source of organisational authority.

---

## AI participates

AI systems are expected to:

* analyse
* review
* challenge
* improve
* identify inconsistencies
* detect drift

AI is considered a peer reviewer rather than an authority.

---

# Intellectual Property

The Knowledge Pipeline contains intellectual property belonging to OSkey.

Knowledge should be treated as a strategic business asset regardless of whether it exists as:

* source code
* architecture
* workflows
* engineering corpus
* operational knowledge
* AI prompts
* contracts
* charters
* runtime definitions

Protection of knowledge should extend beyond traditional source code.

---

# Confidentiality

Information should be classified according to sensitivity.

Initial working classifications include:

## Public

Approved for unrestricted sharing.

---

## Internal

Suitable for authorised employees and approved AI environments.

---

## Confidential

Restricted to approved personnel and approved execution environments.

---

## Restricted

Highly sensitive information including credentials, production secrets, customer data and regulated information.

Restricted information should not be processed by general-purpose AI systems without explicit governance.

---

# AI Execution Environments

Different AI environments provide different trust boundaries.

Examples include:

* Local execution
* Approved cloud environments
* Enterprise AI platforms
* Future internal orchestration

Knowledge classification should determine where information may be processed.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The knowledge corpus is intentionally designed to evolve.

Constructive review is encouraged from:

* Product
* Engineering
* QA
* Operations
* Security
* AI systems

Improvements should strengthen knowledge rather than merely increase documentation.

---

# Architectural Drift

Knowledge should evolve alongside the production platform.

Changes introduced through engineering, product development or operational learning should be evaluated for incorporation into the knowledge corpus.

The objective is continual alignment with production reality.

---

# Trust

Trust is established through:

* evidence
* transparency
* review
* repeatability
* accountability

Trust should never depend upon a particular AI model, vendor or technology platform.

---

# Ownership

Knowledge belongs to the organisation.

Individual contributors, whether human or AI, contribute towards its continual improvement.

Knowledge should remain portable between tools, vendors and future AI platforms.

---

# Long-Term Vision

The Knowledge Pipeline should become the trusted organisational memory of OSkey.

It should support:

* humans
* local AI
* cloud AI
* autonomous agents
* governance processes
* future technologies

while preserving confidentiality, intellectual property and organisational trust.

---

# Definition of Success

This charter will be considered successful when:

* knowledge is treated as a strategic asset
* intellectual property is intentionally protected
* confidentiality is governed through policy rather than assumption
* AI participation strengthens organisational understanding
* humans remain accountable for governance
* knowledge continues to improve regardless of which AI technologies are adopted in the future.

---

> *"Knowledge is created by many, validated by evidence, governed by humans, and shared responsibly with both people and AI."*
